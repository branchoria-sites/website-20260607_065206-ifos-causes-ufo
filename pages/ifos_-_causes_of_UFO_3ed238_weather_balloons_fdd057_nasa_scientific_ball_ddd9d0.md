## Introduction

Among balloon-related explanations for UFO reports, NASA’s scientific balloon programme occupies a special place because its aircraft-like payloads can look far stranger than ordinary weather balloons. A routine weather balloon is usually perceived as a small sphere with a modest instrument package beneath it. By contrast, a NASA research balloon may be hundreds of feet across at altitude and suspend a complex assembly of telescopes, antennas, solar panels, cables, parachutes and instrument platforms weighing several tonnes. From the ground, that multi-part structure can create silhouettes that resemble discs, crosses, jellyfish, floating machinery or other unfamiliar forms. ## The Giant Balloons That Do Look Weird

NASA’s scientific balloons are designed as near-space research platforms rather than simple atmospheric probes. The programme supports payloads ranging from a few pounds to roughly 8,000 pounds and can carry observatory-class instruments to altitudes above 120,000 feet, where they operate above most of Earth’s [atmosphere]({{ 'atmosphere/' | relative_url }}). Some balloon envelopes can reach about 140 metres in diameter when fully expanded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Scientific_Balloon_Facility" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Columbia Scientific Balloon Facility</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Columbia Scientific Balloon Facility</span></span></span><span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/scientificballoons/mediaresources/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Scientific Balloons Media ResourcesThe largest of NASA&#x27;s scientific balloons, the super pressure balloon provides long-duration fligh...</span></span></span><span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ipac.caltech.edu/irspace/NASA%20Scientific%20Balloons.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ipac.caltech.edu">[IPAC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ipac.caltech.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA Balloon ProgramThe flight system at launch includes a balloon, a parachute and a payload that includes the scientific instrument...</span></span></span>

## How Scientific Balloons Differ from Weather Balloons

The most important difference is that a NASA scientific balloon is intended to be a flying observatory. Research missions have carried [infrared]({{ 'infrared/' | relative_url }}) telescopes, cosmic-ray detectors, astronomy instruments and other large scientific payloads that require substantial structural support.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.mdpi.com/2226-4310/10/11/960" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mdpi.com">[MDPI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mdpi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Data Downloaded via Parachute from a NASA Super...by EL Sirks · 2023 · Cited by 9 — In April 2023, the superBIT telescope was lifted...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 2023</span></span></span>

A standard weather balloon generally carries a small radiosonde package. A scientific mission may instead fly a gondola that resembles a skeletal spacecraft frame. Some astronomy projects suspend full telescope systems beneath the balloon, complete with precision pointing equipment capable of maintaining arc-second accuracy while observing distant celestial targets.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">uap independent study team final report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Study Team ReportObservations of objects in our skies that cannot be identified as balloons, aircraft, or natural known phenomena have be...</span></span></span>

## Payloads, Parachutes, and Multi-Part Silhouettes

Many reports of strange airborne shapes arise because observers are unknowingly viewing several connected components at once.

A scientific balloon’s parachute can be extremely prominent. Between the balloon and the gondola hangs a long assembly of cables and hardware. Depending on the viewing angle, the parachute may appear as a separate object, a glowing canopy, a ring-like structure or an extension of the payload itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ipac.caltech.edu/irspace/NASA%20Scientific%20Balloons.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ipac.caltech.edu">[IPAC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ipac.caltech.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA Balloon ProgramThe flight system at launch includes a balloon, a parachute and a payload that includes the scientific instrument...</span></span></span>

The gondola adds another layer of complexity. Balloon-borne telescopes and detectors are frequently built from lightweight open frameworks rather than streamlined housings. From a distance, these frameworks can resemble geometric shapes with protrusions extending in multiple directions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Mogul" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Mogul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Mogul</span></span></span>

Lighting conditions can exaggerate the effect. The balloon may be brightly illuminated while the gondola remains dark. At other times the gondola flashes in sunlight while the balloon itself is difficult to see. Witnesses may therefore report:

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A bright object with a dark craft beneath it.
* A glowing disc with a trailing appendage.
* A cross-shaped or star-shaped object.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.instagram.com/reel/DNI6G20K08d/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: instagram.com">[instagram.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">instagram.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UF O / UAP, Balloons are in the first part of the video</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The newly released UFO Files contain footage of a star-shaped object moving side to side in ways that don&#x27;t resemble any known human-made...</span></span></span>
* A jellyfish-like form suspended in the sky.
* Multiple apparently connected objects moving together.

</div>

These descriptions sound unusual, yet they are consistent with known scientific balloon configurations viewed under difficult visual conditions.

### Why the Shape Seems to Change

Unlike a rigid aircraft, a balloon system is flexible.

The balloon envelope expands dramatically as it climbs. Suspension lines shift position. Parachutes can partially inflate or fold. The gondola may rotate slowly beneath the flight train. Even small changes in orientation can produce large apparent changes in shape when viewed from tens of kilometres away.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ipac.caltech.edu/irspace/NASA%20Scientific%20Balloons.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ipac.caltech.edu">[IPAC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ipac.caltech.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA Balloon ProgramThe flight system at launch includes a balloon, a parachute and a payload that includes the scientific instrument...</span></span></span>

A witness observing only intermittently may therefore believe the object is morphing or changing configuration when it is simply rotating or presenting a different angle to the observer.

## Why Research Flights Can Become Public Mysteries

Scientific balloon launches are far less common than daily weather-balloon operations, which means many people have never seen one.

## What Makes a Balloon Silhouette Different from a Conventional Aircraft

One reason scientific balloons generate confusion is that they violate expectations about how aircraft should look and move.

Aircraft generally have wings, a fuselage, propulsion and predictable lighting patterns. Scientific balloons often display none of these characteristics.
